# Event Policy — Design (seed)

IRL events must be **genuine community gatherings for real-world connection.** Event policy defines what belongs — protecting the community from being repurposed (dating, sales) and keeping IRL true to its identity. It's foundational (connects to the user agreement, Group 1) and its primary enforcement surface is the **organiser flow** (`organizer-engagement.md`). **This note seeds the area.**

## What IRL is for (the positive frame)

Activity-anchored community gatherings, interest and skill-sharing groups, welcoming oriented events — anything whose purpose is people connecting in real life around a shared activity or interest.

## Not allowed

- **Dating / romantic matchmaking as the primary purpose.** IRL is explicitly *not* a dating platform — no messaging, first names, show-up-only. This is identity-defining, not merely a rule.
- **Business, sales, promotion, MLM, recruiting.** IRL is not a marketing channel; events whose primary purpose is to sell or recruit don't belong.
- **Unlawful or hostile exclusionary listings** — the demographic piece (`user-model.md` → *Demographic affinity lives on the event*; `organizer-engagement.md`). Oriented/welcoming framing is fine; hostile or unlawful exclusion is not.
- **Unsafe / illegal / regulated-without-compliance** activities.

## Grey areas (need a stance)

The working line is: **gathering around a shared interest = fine; using IRL to sell, recruit, or convert = not.** Cases to decide:

- **Faith communities** — a shared-faith *social* gathering (fine?) vs. proselytising (not?).
- **Political / advocacy** — an interest group with a leaning (fine?) vs. campaigning / recruiting (not?).
- **Fundraisers / charity** — community-good gatherings (fine?) vs. solicitation (not?).
- **Professional / networking** — a community-of-practice social (fine?) vs. business development (not?).
- **Paid / ticketed** events (a class with a fee) — where's the line vs. commercial?

## Enforcement

Through the organiser flow, gently, with the framing principle (pair every decline with a path — never a bare "no", never implying bad intent):

- **At propose time** — organiser engagement flags an off-policy event ("this reads like a sales event — IRL is for community gatherings; want to reshape it, or it may fit better elsewhere?") and declines clear violations.
- **Reactively** — attendee reports, extending the conduct/safety path to policy violations ("this was actually a pitch").
- **Admin** — edge cases and appeals (Group 4).
