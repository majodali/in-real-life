# Event Policy — Design (seed)

IRL events must be **genuine community gatherings for real-world connection.** Event policy defines what belongs. It's foundational (connects to the user agreement, Group 1), and its enforcement runs through the **organiser flow** (`organizer-engagement.md`) at propose time and the **attendee debrief** (`debrief.md`) reactively. **This note seeds the area.**

## The line: honesty and non-coercion, not subject matter

The core principle: **IRL is lenient about what an event is *about*, and firm about two things — that it's *honestly described* and *non-coercive*.** We don't police viewpoints; we protect attendees from bait-and-switch and pressure.

This matters because the real failure mode isn't a stated proselytising/campaigning/sales event (few would attend one, so few are posted) — it's an organiser **obscuring** that intent behind an innocent-sounding listing, which is already common practice on other channels. Policing topics wouldn't catch that and would drag IRL into arbitrating ideology. Requiring honesty and catching coercion does catch it, and keeps IRL neutral.

## Open by default

Welcome as genuine gatherings, honestly described and non-coercive — IRL does not gatekeep the subject:

- **Faith communities** — a shared-faith social gathering is fine (proselytising-as-the-real-purpose just needs to be honest about itself, and attendees self-select).
- **Political / advocacy / campaigning / enlistment** — an interest or cause gathering is fine; we don't police the cause.
- **Professional / networking** — a community-of-practice social is fine.
- **Fundraisers / charity** — community-good gatherings are fine.

The leniency is deliberate: gatekeeping viewpoints would betray the bridging mission and put IRL in an untenable arbiter role.

## Not allowed

- **Dating / romantic matchmaking as the primary purpose.** Identity-defining — IRL is the anti-dating-app (no messaging, first names, show-up-only).
- **Deception** — an event materially misrepresenting its purpose (a "hiking meetup" that's really a recruitment drive). The sin is the deception, not the topic.
- **High-pressure recruitment or sales** — coercive tactics, regardless of cause or price.
- **IRL as a commercial / customer-acquisition channel** — profit-making ventures using IRL to acquire customers. (Cost-recovery is fine — see *Money*.)
- **Unlawful or hostile exclusionary listings** (`user-model.md` → *Demographic affinity lives on the event*).
- **Unsafe / illegal / regulated-without-compliance** activities.

## Money

- **Cost-recovery is fine** — donations, a suggested donation, or a fee that plausibly covers real costs (venue, food, materials).
- **The line is purpose, not price** — connection with an incidental fee is fine; a transaction/profit/recruitment wrapped in a gathering is not. A $10 sales pitch is still banned (the pitch, not the price); an honest $15 cost-recovery dinner is fine.
- **Soft heuristic** — small fixed prices (~$5–$10, more where real material costs justify it) are presumed cost-recovery; higher prices invite a gentle "what's the fee covering?" The number is a flag, not a rule; the exact threshold is open.
- **External / third-party ticketed events** get more leniency — IRL is *listing* what exists (a community-theatre ticket, a paid class at a real venue), not the seller. Still no pure commercial promotion.

## Enforcement

Lenient and reactive, not gatekeeping — the (a)–(e) model:

- **(a) Clear policy** — organisers see it plainly at propose time.
- **(b) Encourage honesty** — the organiser flow nudges clear, accurate wording, and stating any cost upfront (the framing principle: help, don't lecture).
- **(c) Lenient by default** — we don't pre-police topic; most events just go.
- **(d) Attendee feedback** — the debrief lets attendees flag an event that was **not as described** or involved **high-pressure recruitment/sales** (`debrief.md` → *Policy feedback*). This is the real detector.
- **(e) Follow-up** — organisers are told this feedback is encouraged and that we'll follow up / act if policy isn't followed; repeated violations feed contributor rating (Group 4).
