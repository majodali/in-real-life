# Decision Register

The canonical record of design decisions for IRL. Each entry is summarized here with a pointer to the note that holds the full reasoning — the notes are where context lives; this register is the index.

**Decisions are revisable.** Every entry is a current best call, not a permanent commitment — especially before we have real user interactions and results. The *process* for reviewing this register and changing the system in response (small tweaks to large pivots) is itself first-class work, deliberately deferred for now — see Governance below. Known weaknesses, gaps, and inconsistencies from the consolidation critique are tracked in `open-risks.md` (corrections applied + decisions still needed).

| D47 | **Affinity edges are consumed strength-weighted, never boolean** (resolves open-risks #19): each directed tap carries its tapper's generosity weight (the shared H2 transform, incl. small-N shrinkage); a mutual's initial strength = its weaker side (the less selective tapper's weight); repeated chosen co-attendance — above the calendar's chance rate — raises strength regardless (observed beats inferred, D7); tap-derived strength decays faster than co-attendance-confirmed strength; crew seeding accumulates weighted strength. **Confirmation is guarded**: do-not-interact zeroes the pair's affinity nudge outright, and one-sided engagement caps confirmation gain (a follower can't launder pursuit into affinity). | D50 | **Protective blocks are hard, immediate, and above every other mechanism.** A block is a hard constraint overriding everything, including the inclusion floor (the floor injects within the feasible set). **Victim-initiated, proof-free, instant, silent** (the blocked person is never notified); **presence-shielding, not event-hiding** — the blocker vanishes from the blocked person's view on every surface, while the blocked person's event visibility is never conditionally altered (no negative-space leak). The protective effect is never gated on review; human review governs only *consequences to the blocked* (enforcement, restriction, removal). Tier line drawn by **need, not relationship type** — no member proves they qualify. Frivolous/weaponized blocking is met with pattern monitoring + human review, never by weakening protection (asymmetric costs: a weakened block risks physical harm and legal exposure; an unwarranted one costs almost nothing). **Block hardness is a commitment, not a hypothesis — no kill criterion.** Full mechanism is Group 4 (open-risks #23) | `matching.md`, `user-model.md` |
| D51 | **The supply loop is a standing function at every scale, never a thin-calendar fallback** (resolves open-risks #22): matching allocates supply and **never relaxes fit to fill the floor** — supply, not standards, is the corrective lever. | D52 | **Protective-block mechanism v1** (resolves open-risks #23): the **rendered-world rule** — for the blocked person, IRL renders a world in which the blocker doesn't exist (all surfaces, counts adjusted to match; indistinguishable from having left IRL), while the blocked person's own experience is never conditionally altered (no negative-space leak). The blocker keeps **ordinary member power only** with a capture-time **awareness / peace choice** (awareness default — surprise is the danger) and one narrow notification, the **itinerary alert** (own confirmed events only); nothing broader in v1. Active placement hard-filtered; browse and attendance rights intact (D10). **Displacement handled**: a blocker's yield after the blocked commits never dents her reliability (auto-quarantined), and displacement/proximity patterns route to human safety review in both directions — never a legitimacy test on the block. **Co-presence is the fiction's stated limit** (reveals the block's existence, never plans; disclosed at capture). RSVP-ing to a blocked *organiser's* event is an informed, per-event choice that necessarily reveals roster presence. **Block ≠ accusation** — no rating/conduct effect by itself; only a separate optional report enters safety review (this is what makes proof-free defensible). Pair signal zeroed at the interpretation layer; no decay or expiry, blocker-liftable only; **environment-local** (D42 unqualified — migration is workshop→production onboarding only; revisit if that widens). Not a hypothesis — no kill criteria; evolves via advocate-informed review only. **Advocate validation planned before build** | `protective-blocks.md`, `matching.md`, `user-model.md`, `debrief.md` |
| D53 | **External events: steward, not organizer; confirmations are mutual commitments between members.** The poster of an external event is its **steward** ("Listed by", never "Organized by") — responsible for keeping the listing current or cancelling it, holding the existing organizer gates (edit/cancel); stewardship is deliberately light so sharing a flyer never feels like owning the event. **No open editing** (any-member update is a griefing surface and drags in confirmation workflow/messaging); the correction channel is the existing **suggestion system** ("moved to 7pm per their site" → steward adopts), with unadopted suggestions doubling as the stale-steward signal (admin surfacing Group 4; "claim stewardship" transfer is the future add if absence bites — never open-edit). **Confirming attendance is allowed and encouraged**: the commitment is to *other members* ("you won't walk in alone"), social not administrative — confirmers gain no edit power. External events are **born `planned`** (a real event needs no proposal phase; full time/place trio required at listing), threshold/auto-plan don't apply (it happens regardless), capacity is informational only (not IRL's to enforce), cost disclosure (D34) applies, attendee debrief unchanged, no organizer-debrief; not-as-described flags point at the listing/steward and feed contributor rating later | `external-events.md`, `event-policy.md`, `organizer-engagement.md` |
| D54 | **Finding each other at the venue: recognition text now; anything interactive is a tracked design area, and proximity tech is constrained by D50/D52.** Every event (all sources) carries an optional **meeting-spot / how-to-recognize-us hint** ("back tables, look for the blue scarf") — the zero-tech answer that covers most cases, live now. | D56 | **Every event gets a machine-readable shape, LLM-extracted at propose time and organizer-correctable.** One `event-shape` call (D37 seam; deterministic stub in workshop/test) classifies the listing into `activityTags` (≤5 free noun phrases), `structure` (structured / semi-structured / unstructured), and `doors` (useful / make-learn / connect); the result rides in `EventProposed.data.shape` frozen at command time and projects onto the event row. | D57 | **Crews v1: triads of mutual-strong pairs, formed from weighted co-attendance, consumed as a capped gathering bonus.** Formation (async projector, incremental): a crew exists when all three pairs of a triad are mutual-strong — both directions tapped positive AND reciprocal met counts ≥ `crewMutualMetPivot` (the D47 co-attendance proxy; never tap counts or boolean mutuals — a spam tapper cannot seed a crew). | D58 | **The comfort envelope gets a machine-comparable form: coarse 3-position scales alongside the story, never instead of it.** Five dimensions (`groupSize`, `structure`, `familiarity`, `role`, `novelty`) each gain an optional `position` on a 3-value scale plus an optional `edgeToward` pole (the structured twin of the growth edge); vocabulary lives in one module (`lib/envelope.mjs`) that extraction, debrief shifts, corrections, and fit all validate against. **Sign-off reasoning recorded**: the scales use the design's inbuilt vocabulary to drive inbuilt functionality, not classification for its own sake; the analysis orders **events per member, never people against each other** (people-scoring is where categorization becomes the problem); fit **orders, never blocks**; and the mechanism is fully transparent to the member. | D59 | **Model legibility: the member sees and corrects what IRL believes about them.** `GET /me/model` renders Layer 2 member-facing — positions, growth edges, doors, interests, strengths, barriers, constraints, each with provenance as plain language ("you told us" / "we've noticed") and the latest story behind it. **Deliberately not behind the agreement re-accept gate** (legibility is a data right, like export; correcting IS participation, so the correction route is gated). **Never shown to anyone, including the member: Layer 3 (affinity, crews, tap stats), contributor rating, any weight or score** — enforced server-side; raw store items never travel. `POST /me/model/correction` emits `UserModelCorrected` (typed: envelope position/edge, interest add/remove, barrier remove — the one-tap dignity path; barriers are never argued with). **Precedence: a correction beats every piece of evidence older than itself, of any provenance; evidence arriving after resumes normal D7. No counters, no expiry clocks** — the recorded philosophy: decay by literal timer is unnatural; the member's activity and experience is the real timer; time doesn't reduce the importance of information, only new grounded information does. **Watch note**: this philosophy sits in tension with spec v4's clock-based affinity/crew half-lives — flagged for future register discussion (evidence-anchored decay), not silently reworked; taken up and resolved as D60 (`evidence-decay.md`, spec v6) | `profile-and-legibility.md`, `user-model.md`, `evidence-decay.md` |

| D60 | **Decay is evidence-based: lived events are the axis, floors are the prior, and silence is never conclusive.** Replaces spec v4/v5's three clock half-lives. The recorded reasoning: a literal timer is a mechanism preferred for ease over validity ("easier to explain" is part of the ease, not a rationale); the evidence set is **known-incomplete** — off-platform contact is invisible, members under-report, and **normalization can reduce in-platform signaling as a tie strengthens** — so the math must respect incompleteness; and absence from a crew signifies ≈ nothing (reconnection routinely feels like no time passed). Mechanism: half-lives count the member's own **debriefed events** (tap strength and crews on the own-activity axis; confirmed strength on **min of both sides'** activity since their own last met-mark — divergence only counts when both are living without each other), and every component decays toward a **floor**, never zero (tap 0, confirmed 0.5, crew 0.5) — below-floor is reserved for grounded counter-evidence (D49 avoidance, D50 blocks). Zero activity → zero decay (the away-and-return member keeps their social graph); recovery on fresh evidence is instant and total; **met-without-tap stays captured-not-used** (normalization is indistinguishable from cooling — promoting it is a spec bump with H4 evidence, never a silent code path). ## Watch-items (emergent effects)

Deliberately *not* solved now, but tracked — a reminder that a system like this always produces unintended consequences and modalities:

- **Gaming via proxies.** Users may learn to use innocuous signals as proxies for an outcome we won't serve directly — e.g. choosing event types (book club, car maintenance) to bias the age/gender/ethnicity mix they encounter. Not addressed now; kept in mind.
- **Creeping categorization.** The chip vocabulary, rules, and sequencing may grow sophisticated enough to amount to a quiet user-categorization system. Categorization itself is fine — *as long as it never dominates user outcomes*. Watch that it stays a tool, not a verdict.
- **Steering dissonance.** The friction between user agency and the boundaries we hold (`debrief.md` → *When a user steers somewhere we won't go*) — needs careful wording and real-world validation.
- **The messaging wedge.** Any gesture channel (kudos, D45) invites reasonable-sounding expansion — "can I just add a short note?" The recorded intention: kudos stays fixed-vocabulary and reply-free; any widening is a full register decision, not a tweak.
- **Goodhart / proxy corruption.** Any success proxy we optimise can be gamed or force-produced (e.g. manufactured crews, inflated want-to-see-again). Treat proxies as *indicators*, triangulate behaviour with self-report, keep humans in the evolution loop (`success-and-progress.md`).
- **Influence concentration.** Charismatic / popular / (unfairly) wealthy members accrue more "likes"; the strawman could let their choices over-steer others' recommendations. Mitigated by the per-member influence cap that doesn't grow with popularity (`matching.md`), but watch it in practice.
- **Aggregate attention concentration.** Many individually-capped, individually-legitimate own-feed nudges can still concentrate demand on a popular member's events — no per-member cap addresses it, because each nudge is one member's own choice. Likely fine; watch demand concentration per organiser/event, especially where it meets finite capacity and composition (`scenario-walkthroughs.md` F3).
- **Covert commercialisation via donations.** "Suggested cost-covering donations" (up to $20 per attendee per occurrence) could become a backdoor to monetising events. Watched by pattern — collection frequency × amount × attendance across an organiser's events, outliers to human review — plus the attendee-debrief detector (`event-policy.md`).
