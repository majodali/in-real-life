Four ways to grow up the UI, without losing the warmth

The brief: a more sophisticated visual language that still feels welcoming, low-stress, comforting. Below is where the UI stands today, then four directions — each applied to the same two screens (the feed and one interview card) so they can be compared honestly. Everything uses real app content; nothing here touches the functionality work in flight.

Where it is today

The bones are good: the earthy palette and the Playfair/DM Sans pairing already say calm, human, unhurried. What holds it back is craft, not concept — type is set small (10–15px nearly everywhere), every card wears identical chrome (white box, 14px radius, accent stripe), there's a single elevation level, and four accent colors compete on one screen. It reads as a capable prototype rather than a considered product.

Worth keeping

  • The palette's temperament — greens and warm neutrals, nothing loud.
  • Serif greeting + humanist sans body: the voice already sounds right.
  • Small, calm status pills instead of counters and badges.

What limits it

  • Type scale is timid — the greeting is the only moment of scale; body sits at 11–13px.
  • One card treatment for every kind of content; the accent stripe carries no meaning.
  • Sage, amber, rust and moss all appear on one screen; nothing gets to be the accent.
  • The dark header block is the heaviest element on screen but holds the least information.

A Morning Linen

The current identity, recrafted rather than replaced. Same world — cream, moss, a single amber note — but with a real typographic scale, one soft elevation system, and air. The dark header goes; the greeting becomes the hero, set large in Playfair with an italic turn. Cards lose the accent stripe and earn a quiet border, deep-but-diffuse shadow, and room to breathe. If the current UI is a sketch, this is the same drawing, inked.

Playfair Display for greetings & titles (larger, with italics) · DM Sans body at 14–15px · pills over stripes · one shadow recipe

Keeps

  • The whole documented palette, refined by a degree, not swapped.
  • Playfair + DM Sans — the pairing users have already met.

Changes

  • Light header: the greeting owns the screen; chrome recedes.
  • Time moves into an amber pill — the one warm note per card.
  • Avatar clusters replace the "going" counter pill: people, not numbers.
  • Base type up to 14–15px; titles up to 19px.

Watch out

  • Lowest-risk option — the risk is that it's only a refinement if the craft isn't held everywhere.

B Field Notes

A naturalist's journal, not an app. Cards disappear entirely: the feed becomes a set week-list — date numerals in a column, hairline rules, small-caps metadata, Fraunces carrying titles and italic asides. The single ochre note marks what's recommended. It borrows the quiet authority of print: nothing pops, nothing pushes, and there is visibly nothing to scroll forever through — which is exactly the product's promise.

Fraunces (roman + italic) for titles & asides · DM Sans small-caps for metadata · rules instead of boxes · ink on paper

  • Warm ground, green-and-ochre restraint, the unhurried voice.

Changes

  • No cards, no shadows — hierarchy comes entirely from type and rules.
  • Date numerals become the feed's visual anchor; scanning by day is instant.

  • Editorial one-liners want authored copy (the AI spine could write them).

C Lantern

The app as a porch light. A deep pine ground with one candlelit amber accent — your confirmed plan literally glows; everything else rests. Meetups get planned in the evening on the couch, and a dark, warm surface is the lowest-stress reading environment there is. Shown here as a full direction; it also works as the evening companion theme to A or B.

  • Amber is rationed to one job: marking your commitments.
  • Elevation via glow and border, never grey shadow.

D Pebble

The furthest step: leave the cream-and-serif world for a coastal one — fog, sea glass, kelp, a little clay. Fraunces at a soft semibold gives it a friendly, rounded confidence; surfaces are big smooth stones (26px radii, one feather-soft shadow); every action is a pill. This is the "calm modern wellness" register done with restraint — closer to what people expect a polished 2026 app to feel like, at the cost of the current identity.

Keeps

  • Green as the anchor hue; softness as the core feeling.
  • DM Sans body — continuity in the reading voice.

Changes

  • Palette cools toward sea glass and fog; cream exits.
  • Fraunces SemiBold replaces Playfair — rounder, more contemporary.
  • Facts become chips; actions become pills; radii grow to 26px.

Watch out

  • Closest neighbor to generic "calm app" territory; the Fraunces voice and coastal palette are what keep it specific.

Side by side

All four hold the brief — welcoming, low-stress, comforting. They differ in how far they move from today and where the sophistication comes from.

DirectionFeels likeSophistication fromDistance from todayBest if
A · Morning Linen A well-kept sunroom Scale, spacing, one elevation system Small — a css refactor, same tokens refined You like the identity and want it executed properly
B · Field Notes A naturalist's journal Print typography; removing chrome entirely Medium — same palette family, new layout grammar You want the most distinctive, most "anti-app" statement
C · Lantern A porch light at dusk Darkness, restraint, one rationed glow Medium — inverted palette, same structure Evening use dominates, or as the night theme of A/B
D · Pebble Beach stones, sea glass Soft-modern precision: pills, big radii, one shadow Large — new palette and display face; a rebrand You want a contemporary reset while it's still cheap to do

Recommendation

Build A as the system, steal from B, keep C in the drawer as the dark theme.

Morning Linen preserves everything the docs and existing users already encode about the brand while fixing the actual gap — craft. Fold in Field Notes' best idea where the content is list-like: the date-anchored entry treatment is a genuinely better feed grammar than a stack of identical cards, and the two combine naturally (linen surfaces for things you act on, journal rules for things you scan). Lantern then arrives later as the `prefers-color-scheme: dark` companion — its palette is already a clean inversion of A's.

Pebble is the honest alternative, not a compromise: if you feel the cream-serif world is too familiar to carry the product long-term, the moment to switch is now, pre-launch. That's a brand decision more than a UI one.

Regardless of direction

These moves are already reflected in every mockup above and would go into the spec whichever way you choose:

  • Raise the type floor. Body 14–15px, titles 18–19px, metadata never below 11px. The single biggest "sophistication" lever available.
  • One accent per screen. Amber (or ochre, or sea glass) gets one job — marking your commitments or the recommendation — and nothing else competes.
  • One elevation recipe. A single border + shadow (or rule, or glow) treatment, everywhere; delete the per-card accent stripes.
  • People over counters. Avatar clusters instead of "4 going" pills wherever roster data exists — it's a people app.
  • Lighten the header. The greeting is the hero; the dark header block goes (except in Lantern, where dark is the ground).
  • Calm motion. 150–200ms ease-out fades and small translates only; full respect for prefers-reduced-motion.

Next step: pick a direction (or a blend) and I'll turn it into a full spec — design tokens, component inventory, and a working restyle of styles.css behind the existing class names, so it lands without touching the functionality work.
