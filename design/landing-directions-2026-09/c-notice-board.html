A plain way to meet your neighbors in person.

in·real·life is a short list of small meetups near where you live — a walk, a book swap, a coffee — and a way to say you'll be there. Then you go. There is nothing else to it, and that is deliberate.

How it works

Four steps, in this order.

  1. Sign up, and we check you're local

    Tell us where you live and give us a way to reach you. We confirm you're on Bainbridge before anything else happens. That is done by us, not by a machine, so allow a few days.

  2. A short interview, in your own words

    What you enjoy, when you're free, the kind of company you like. A few minutes. It's how we know what's worth showing you.

  3. See what's coming up nearby

    A short list of meetups close to where you live, and who has said they'll be there by first name. That is the whole screen.

  4. Say you'll be there, then be there

    Confirming is a small promise: someone is expecting you. Hellos happen at the meetup, not before it.

Rules of the board

What we don't do, and what we do instead.

We don'tInstead
We don't have messaging.
There is no inbox and no direct messages. To talk to someone, go where they'll be. Every conversation that starts here happens face to face.
We don't show last names.
A first name and enough about you to be recognized when you arrive. Your last name, address and phone number stay yours.
We don't have a feed, likes, follows or scores.
You see the meetups coming up and the first names of who's coming. Nothing is liked, ranked in public, or designed to keep you scrolling.
We don't nudge.
No streaks, no "it's been a while", no notification you can't switch off. If we contact you, it is about a meetup you're part of, and you choose how.
We don't run ads, and we don't sell what we know about you.
We are a not-for-profit. We keep the little a meetup needs — a first name, a locality, when you're free — and it goes no further.
We don't let the whole internet in.
Adults, 18 and over, who live here. We check locality at sign-up so the place stays the size of a neighborhood. If you're somewhere else, we'll say so kindly and tell you when we reach you.

Where we operate

Bainbridge Island, Washington. That is the whole list for now.

We add places one at a time, and only when the last one is standing on its own. Each should stay small enough to feel like a neighborhood rather than a network.

Not on Bainbridge? You can still leave your name at sign-up. We'll write when we get to your neighborhood, and not before.

Who runs it

A small not-for-profit. There is no press page and no office to visit. Questions, complaints, or a copy of your data: hello@in-real.life. We are few, so allow a week.

Every decision about how this works is written down and public: How we decide.
