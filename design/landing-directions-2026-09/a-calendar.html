What the cards leave out, on purpose

You have just read ten meetups without seeing how many people are going, what anyone looks like, or which one is "popular". That is the design. Each thing we leave out is paired with what we do instead.

  • No headcounts

    We never show how many people said yes, and no meetup is "filling up". Instead: who is hosting, where to stand and what to bring, which is what you need to decide.

  • No faces, no surnames

    There are no profile photos to size people up by. Instead: a first name and one line about how to spot the host. Your surname, address and phone number stay yours.

  • No inbox

    There is no messaging and no direct messages, so there is nothing to check and nobody can write to you. Instead: the way to talk to someone is to go where they said they would be, and ask in person.

  • No ranking

    Nothing is trending, promoted or scored, and we do not rank people. Instead: the list runs in date order, and a quiet meetup sits at the same height as a busy one.

  • Nothing to keep up with

    No streaks, no badges, no feed that never ends. Miss a month and nothing happens to you. Instead: a week of meetups, then the week ends.

  • No ads, nothing for sale

    in·real·life is not for profit. We do not sell advertising, and we do not sell what we know about you. Instead: we collect the minimum a meetup needs, and we publish how we decide things.

How it works

Three steps. The first one is the part that makes the room trustworthy.

  1. Sign up, and show us you're local

    Sign-up is for adults, 18 and over, and we verify that you live on the island before you see anyone. That is what makes this a neighbourhood and not the whole internet.

    Then a short interview: what you enjoy, when you are free, the kind of company you like.

  2. Pick something from the week

    Read the calendar the way you read the one above. When something looks right, say you'll be there. That is a promise to the host, not a like.

  3. Show up

    Look for the first name on the card and say hello. That is where the app stops and the meetup starts.

    Afterwards we ask one thing: was it worth another go?

Where it works

We are starting on Bainbridge Island, Washington, and only there. We add neighbourhoods one at a time, so each one stays small enough to recognise itself.

Not on the island? You can still sign up. We will let you know when we reach your neighbourhood, and we will not write to you in between.

The real calendar has no first week yet.

Sign up, look at what is there, and if it is empty, propose the first thing. A walk is enough.
