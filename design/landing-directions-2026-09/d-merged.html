To our neighbors on Bainbridge

You're invited to meet your neighbors, in real life.

in·real·life is a community-driven way to find meetups on Bainbridge Island and to say you'll be there. A coffee walk, a book swap, a low-tide morning. You confirm, and then you show up. That is the whole thing.

At a glance

What it is
A short list of small, in-person meetups near where you live, and a way to say you'll come.
Who it's for
Adults, 18 and over, living on Bainbridge Island. Sign-up takes a few minutes; then we check you're local.
Who runs it
It's community-driven: neighbors propose the meetups, and every decision about how it works is public. Not for profit, no ads, nothing for sale.
Where
Bainbridge Island, Washington, to begin with. One place at a time after that.

What the cards leave out, on purpose

You have just read ten meetups without seeing what anyone looks like or which one is "popular". That is the design. Each thing we leave out is paired with what we do instead.

  • No faces, no surnames

    There are no profile photos to size people up by. Instead: a first name, and a line from the host on how to find the group. Your surname, address and phone number stay yours.

  • No inbox

    There is no messaging and no direct messages, so there is nothing to check and nobody can write to you. Instead: the way to talk to someone is to go where they said they would be, and ask in person.

  • No popularity contest

    Nothing is trending or promoted, and we never rank people. Instead: meetups are suggested by fit — what you enjoy, when you're free, the size of group you like — and never by how many are going.

  • Nothing to keep up with

    No streaks, no badges, no feed that never ends. Miss a month and nothing happens to you. Instead: a short list of what's coming up nearby, there when you want it.

  • No ads, nothing for sale

    in·real·life is not for profit. We do not sell advertising, and we do not sell what we know about you. Instead: we collect the minimum a meetup needs, and we publish how we decide things.

How it works

Three steps. The first one is the part that makes the room trustworthy.

  1. Sign up, and show us you're local

    Sign-up is for adults, 18 and over, and we verify that you live on the island before you see anyone. That is what makes this a neighborhood and not the whole internet.

    Then a short interview: what you enjoy, when you are free, the kind of company you like.

  2. Pick something coming up

    Read the real calendar the way you read the ideas above. When something looks right, say you'll be there. That is a promise to the host, not a like.

  3. Show up

    Look for the first name on the card and say hello. That is where the app stops and the meetup starts.

    Afterwards we ask how it went, starting with whether it was worth another go.

Where it works

We are starting on Bainbridge Island, Washington, and only there. We add neighborhoods one at a time, so each one stays small enough to recognize itself.

Not on the island? Leave your email at sign-up. We will let you know when we reach your neighborhood, and we will not write to you in between.

We hope to see you there.

Sign up, see what neighbors have proposed, and say you'll come. Or propose something yourself; a walk is enough.
